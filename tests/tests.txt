# Tests

## Test 1 - Consumer account keeps a claim after sign-out [sanity]
Viewport: mobile (375x667)
Covers: persistent consumer registration, password sign-in, claim creation, database persistence, session restoration
Description: Verifies the former anonymous demo path is replaced by a real account whose claim remains available after leaving the session.
Steps:
1. Create a Consumer account with a fresh valid test email and password, then create a claim with merchant, order, product, amount, purchase date, description, and requested resolution.
2. Sign out, then sign back in with the same email and password.
3. Open Dashboard and the previously created claim.
Expected: The account is labeled Consumer account, the claim still appears after sign-out/sign-in, and its stored purchase details remain visible.

## Test 2 - Merchant registration exposes only merchant workflow
Viewport: desktop (1280x800)
Covers: persistent merchant registration, role-aware navigation, merchant dashboard, consumer workflow isolation
Description: Verifies a merchant account is visibly distinct and cannot create consumer claims or product fingerprints.
Steps:
1. Create a Merchant account with a fresh valid test email and password.
2. Inspect the sidebar and dashboard.
3. Refresh the page.
Expected: The sidebar identifies Merchant account, New claim and Product fingerprints are absent, and the merchant dashboard remains available after refresh.

## Test 3 - One email cannot be registered under both roles
Viewport: mobile (375x667)
Covers: duplicate-email guardrail, immutable account role, test registration endpoint
Description: Verifies an email already used for one role cannot be reused to create the opposite role.
Setup:
1. Create a Consumer account with a fresh test email and password, then sign out.
Steps:
1. Open Create account and select Merchant.
2. Enter the same email with any valid password and submit.
3. Sign in using the original consumer credentials.
Expected: Merchant registration is rejected with a visible existing-email message, and the original account still appears as Consumer account.

## Test 4 - Merchant invitation rejects a consumer and survives merchant relogin
Viewport: desktop (1280x800)
Covers: merchant invitation role boundary, redeem-invite authorization, merchant case membership, durable merchant dashboard assignment
Description: Verifies invitations cannot convert consumers into merchants and accepted assignments persist for the merchant account.
Setup:
1. Actor A creates a Consumer account, creates a claim, generates its merchant invitation, and copies the invitation link.
2. Actor B creates a separate Merchant account and signs out.
Steps:
1. While signed in as Actor A Consumer, open the merchant invitation and attempt to proceed.
2. Use the invitation flow to sign out and sign in as Actor B Merchant, then accept the claim.
3. Sign Actor B out and back in, then open the merchant dashboard.
Expected: Actor A is not converted to merchant, Actor B can accept the invitation, and the assigned claim remains listed after Actor B signs out and back in.

## Test 5 - Existing claim assistance remains available to a persistent consumer
Viewport: desktop (1280x800)
Covers: persistent claim access, /api/claim-assist, /api/claim-package, merchant support discovery
Description: Verifies the authentication change does not remove the established claim-preparation tools.
Setup:
1. Sign in to a Consumer account with an existing claim that includes a product URL.
Steps:
1. Open the claim and request a neutral AI summary.
2. Prepare a merchant message.
3. Run merchant support discovery from the saved product URL.
Expected: The saved claim loads from the account and the AI/support tools return visible results without requiring a demo session.
